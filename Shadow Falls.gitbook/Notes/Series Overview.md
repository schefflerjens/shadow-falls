# Series Overview

Jam Slate has been keeping Shadow Falls safe for over a century. Clay golem Sheriff, first-person noir narrator. The town's supernaturals and humans coexist under an unspoken veil — locals know, outsiders don't ask.

The last few years were quiet. Peaceful. Petty crime, bar fights, lost cats up trees. Then the murders started. A serial killer targeting townsfolk, staged with theatrical cruelty. Jam chased them down — twins with forgettable faces — and caught them. But a mage teleported them away before he could bring them in.

That opened the floodgates. A Voodoo Loa (Papa Legba) offered a deal. Charlaine got kidnapped. Krabat vanished alongside her. Jam tracked her to a cabin in the woods, fought through a small army of identical flesh-golem guards, and rescued her. But the price Legba demanded was paid in blood — Mama Marie, Charlaine's grandmother, was turned into a vampire to settle the debt.

Now Jam is in deeper than he's ever been. A government agent (Herb Weatherby) has arrived. A decoded map points somewhere. Krabat is still missing. The mage behind the murder twins is still out there. And Legba? He's owed a favor now, and the god of crossroads doesn't give those out for free.

The series is urban fantasy noir. First-person past tense. Jam Slate's voice. Shadow Falls is a small town with a big secret, and every answer comes with a price tag.

## Themes
- Justice at a cost. There's no clean win.
- The weight of longevity — Jam's been alive for over a century. Everyone he gets close to eventually ages, dies, or leaves.
- Found family — Jam, Charlaine, Anna. They're all each other has.
- Dignity in imperfection — recovering vampire, aging golem, skeptical deputy with dormant power. None of them are what they're supposed to be.
